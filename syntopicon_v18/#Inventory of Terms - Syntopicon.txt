Inventory  of  Terms The Inventory of Terms is designed to facilitate the use of
the  Syntopicon  as  a  reference  book.  By  means of the terms listed below in
alphabetical  order,  the  Inventory  directs  us  to the relevant topics in the
chapters and thus enables us to discover what the Great Books have to say on the
3,000  topics comprising the themes of the great conversation. The titles of the
chapters  are included in this alphabetical list because each great idea is also
discussed in chapters other than the one devoted to its consideration .

In  order  to  see  the  particular  topic in the context of related topics, the
reader  should  turn  first  to  the Outline of Topics for the idea in which the
particular  topic  appears.  The  Outline  of Topics in each chapter immediately
precedes  the  Reference  section,  where  the particular topics are listed with
citations to the relevant passages from the Great Books.

Each  term  in  the  Inventory  is accompanied by references to the chapters and
topics  in  which  the term is a significant element. The terms are the words or
phrases  printed  in  boldface  type.  Whole  chapters are cited by both chapter
number  and name; for example, see CH 4: Art. Individual topics are cited by the
name of the chapter and the number of the topic in the Outline of Topics in that
chapter; for example, see Art 5a. In some cases the Inventory listing might be a
cross-reference  to  other places in the Inventory. Cross-references are also in
boldface type.

In  each  entry  topics  are  cited in the alphabetical order of the chapters in
which  they  appear.  However,  whole  chapters,  when cited, always precede the
individual  topics.  In  some entries the citations are divided into two groups,
those  of  primary  and  those  of  secondary  significance.  The first group is
separated from the second by a diagonal line, followed by see also.

Some  words  carry qualifiers in parentheses to indicate the distinct term being
used.  For  example,  Corruption  (moral)  provides  references  that  apply  to
corruption in the moral or ethical sense, whereas Corruption (phys.) pertains to
biological  decay  or decomposition, and Corruption (pol.) to abuses of power in
the sphere of politics.
