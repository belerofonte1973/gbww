Prudence is sometimes described, not as a 
virtue of the mind, or even as the power of

emotional disposition. It is associated with

The cautiousness of the over-deliberate man

When prudence is conceived as excessive

Aristotle and Aquinas would use such facts

Nor are prudence and imprudence simply 
matters of temperament. Men may differ in 
their temperamental dispositions; but, accord-

be confused with virtues and vices. One man 
may be by nature more fearful or fearless than

velop the vices of imprudence, becoming, like 
Hamlet or Nicias, irresolute; or, like Alcibiades, 
impatient of counsel or ill advised, lacking care 
in deliberation and soundness in judgment.
